# paper 2: positioning accuracy from a webcam

## working title

accuracy of webcam landmark detection and rotation measurement for pre-exposure positioning checks in radiography

## research aim

this is the second paper in the imaging copilot program. it establishes whether a single ordinary webcam can detect the anatomical landmarks and measure the body rotation that radiographers use to position patients, well enough to flag positioning errors before exposure. positioning errors are the dominant cause of rejected images, so this paper validates the positioning track of the copilot. it publishes on its own after the synthetic ground truth exists.

## research question (one primary)

what is the accuracy of webcam landmark detection and rotation measurement against known ground truth?

## hypothesis

a webcam with pose and landmark models detects the positioning landmarks (jugular notch for chest, iliac crest for pelvis, both provisional pending confirmation of the landmark set) and measures body rotation about the long axis accurately enough to flag a rotation beyond the department's tolerance before exposure. stated as a testable claim: landmark localization error under a defined threshold in centimeters, and rotation error under a defined threshold in degrees, against ground truth.

## why this question matters

positioning errors cause the majority of rejects in digital radiography. verified in the ledger: 76 percent in serra (v001), 79.4 and 77.3 percent in bantas (v007), positioning and breathing at 66.67 percent of chest rejects in the songklanakarin audit (v008). no published system gives pre-exposure positioning feedback from an ordinary rgb webcam (negative claim from the sweeps, still pending in the ledger). this paper validates the piece that attacks the dominant reject cause.

## method outline

- ground truth source: synthetic scenes built with smpl-x bodies in a simulated room, generated by the blenderproc harness. each scene knows the exact landmark positions and body rotation because it is rendered from a known model pose.
- camera: a virtual webcam in the simulated room at a realistic working position, matching the depth paper's geometry.
- models: pose and landmark detection from the camera frames. exact model choice pending verification of current options and licenses.
- measurements: landmark position in centimeters relative to the detector plane, and rotation about the long axis in degrees.
- comparison: predicted values against the known ground truth from the rendering.
- outcome: landmark localization error in centimeters and rotation error in degrees, reported per projection (chest pa, pelvis ap) and per body habitus.
- reproducibility: every render command, model version, and parameter recorded next to the results.

## ethics position

no human subjects. the bodies are synthetic models in simulated scenes. no ethics approval required. the ethics statement in the paper will say exactly this, that validation used synthetic human models only.

## evaluation protocol reference

this paper reports measurement accuracy only, the ability to detect a positioning error before exposure. it does not yet report reject-rate reduction in a real department. the reject-rate evaluation protocol is a separate document used by the later program work, because a real-department outcome needs the working tool, human subjects, and ethics approval, none of which exist at this stage.

## what success looks like

landmark error and rotation error below the tolerances that departments actually use. the pelvis rotation tolerance and the chest symmetry threshold come from the technique profile examples in the repo, as placeholders until real department values exist. if the synthetic results are strong, the method proceeds to a physical prototype. if not, the positioning approach is revised and the paper reports the honest result.

## trajectory

this paper depends on paper 1 only for the shared geometry and camera assumptions, not on its results. it validates the positioning track. together with paper 1 it establishes the two measurement foundations the copilot needs. the program aim, reject-rate reduction in real use, comes after both.
