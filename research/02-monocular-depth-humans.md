# Monocular RGB depth for patient body thickness

Literature review for the radiography copilot. Question: how accurate is metric or thickness recovery from monocular RGB depth on human bodies, and which techniques improve it? Compiled August 2026 from primary sources (arXiv, proceedings, official repos).

## Summary

Single-image depth models are now good enough that a known reference plane can turn them into a centimeter-scale measuring tool on humans, but only under controlled capture. General-purpose metric models report zero-shot errors equivalent to roughly 3 to 7 percent relative error on indoor benchmarks (AbsRel 0.052 to 0.077, RMSE 0.18 to 0.27 m on NYUv2). Nobody publishes torso-thickness error directly, so the honest estimate comes from adjacent work: anchored photogrammetry systems measure infant length from one phone photo with 1.8 to 2.5 cm mean error uncontrolled and about 1.1 cm in-clinic, sticker-based per-point anchors hit 0.11 cm on a rigid board, and consumer body-scan apps range from 0.5 cm precision error to ±6.5 cm RMSE depending on how seriously they take calibration. The pattern is consistent: relative depth quality is rarely the bottleneck. Scale anchoring, capture discipline, and averaging over frames determine whether you land near 1 cm or near 5 cm. For our bucky-plane plan this is encouraging: we control the scene (fixed wall stand, known detector plane, cooperative pose), which is exactly the regime where the best reported results live. The two real risks are loose clothing and unknown webcam intrinsics, both of which have documented fixes.

Benchmark numbers, zero-shot unless noted:

- Depth Anything V2 trains on synthetic labels plus 62M pseudo-labeled real images, runs more than 10x faster than diffusion-based Marigold, and ships 24.8M (Small) to 1.3B (Giant) parameter variants. Its metric fine-tune lineage reports NYUv2 δ1 0.984, AbsRel 0.056, RMSE 0.206 m versus ZoeDepth's 0.951 / 0.077 / 0.282 m ([metric_depth README](https://github.com/LiheYoung/Depth-Anything/tree/main/metric_depth)).
- Metric3D v2 resolves focal-length ambiguity with a canonical camera space transform trained over 16M images from thousands of camera models. Zero-shot NYUv2: δ1 0.975, AbsRel 0.063, RMSE 0.251 m (ViT-L); fine-tuned NYUv2 RMSE 0.183 m. Zero-shot KITTI AbsRel 0.052. Reported inference speeds on workstation GPUs: ViT-S 11.6 fps at 2.9 GB, ViT-L 9.5 fps at 7.0 GB, giant 5.0 fps at 15.6 GB ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15506), [supp tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15506v4)). It demonstrated single-image metrology (measuring object sizes on phone photos) when EXIF focal length is available.
- Depth Pro produces 2.25 MP metric depth in 0.3 s on a V100, leads on boundary sharpness, and has state-of-the-art zero-shot focal estimation. Its metric accuracy leans indoors: δ1 82.3 on iBims-1 but 29.9 on DDAD in UniDepth's comparison table ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02073v1), [repo](https://github.com/apple/ml-depth-pro)). Note the released checkpoint is a re-trained reference implementation whose performance "does not match exactly".
- UniDepth sidesteps the intrinsics problem by predicting a dense camera representation first, then conditioning depth on it. Zero-shot δ1 from its own cross-model table: NYUv2 98.8 (V2), SUN-RGBD 96.4, iBims-1 94.5, ETH3D 85.2, KITTI 98.9, ranked first on the official KITTI depth prediction leaderboard at submission time ([README table](https://github.com/lpiccinelli-eth/UniDepth), [CVPR page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Piccinelli_UniDepth_Universal_Monocular_Metric_Depth_Estimation_CVPR_2024_paper.html)). V2 adds ONNX export, confidence output, and faster inference. The non-commercial license matters if this ever ships clinically.
- ZoeDepth was the earlier metric-transfer standard (NYUv2 δ1 0.955, REL 0.075, RMSE 0.270 m for ZoeD-M12-N) and is now dated and unmaintained ([paper table](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12288v1), [repo notice](https://github.com/isl-org/ZoeDepth)).

Takeaway for us: relative models (Depth Anything family) plus our own plane anchor compete with, and often beat, built-in metric heads once a reference exists, and they carry the friendliest licenses and smallest footprints.

## 2. Accuracy on humans specifically

No benchmark publishes monocular-depth torso-thickness error in cm. Three lines of evidence bracket it.

Human-centric depth reconstruction:

- Self-supervised human depth from monocular video (SMPL base shape plus residual detail) reaches MAE around 2.6 to 2.9 cm against ground-truth meshes on multi-view test sets ([Tan et al., CVPR 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Tan_Self-Supervised_Human_Depth_Estimation_From_Monocular_Videos_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf)). This is multi-frame and human-tailored, so treat it as an upper bound on what generic single-frame models achieve on bodies.
- Neural surface fields fitted to Kinect RGB-D sequences reconstruct clothed bodies at 0.65 to 0.92 cm Chamfer distance ([NSF, ICCV 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Xue_NSF_Neural_Surface_Fields_for_Human_Modeling_from_Monocular_Depth_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)), showing that with decent input depth the human-shape prior cleans up sensor noise well below 1 cm.
- A 2016 study estimated full-body anthropometrics from a single consumer depth image with average error 1.9 cm at real-time rates ([IS&T EI 2016](https://library.imaging.org/ei/articles/28/21/art00016)).
- A 2026 cross-domain benchmark evaluated 69 monocular depth models against human distance judgments on KITTI and NYU scenes and found high-accuracy models converge toward similar residual error structures rather than perfect agreement with physical depth ([arXiv 2512.08163](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08163v2)). Useful context: even the best models retain systematic biases, which argues for calibrating them against our own rig rather than trusting factory metrics.

Body dimensions from ordinary RGB cameras:

- Infant recumbent length from a single smartphone photo plus an 85.6×54.0 mm reference card: bias 0.03 cm, mean absolute error 2.47 cm per image, improving to 1.77 cm when averaging nine or more images; 95.4 percent of single predictions within 10 percent of truth. That approaches interobserver technical error in general clinics (1.25 to 1.59 cm) ([LAI tool, JMIR Pediatrics 2024](https://pediatrics.jmir.org/2024/1/e59564/); follow-up validation in [BMJ Health Care Informatics 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjhci-2025-101540)).
- Newborn height from a single photo with a clinic-trained MobileNet regression reached MAE under 1.1 cm across 200 newborns ([Shu et al., CVPRW 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/CVPM/papers/Shu_Single_Image_Based_Infant_Body_Height_and_Weight_Estimation_CVPRW_2023_paper.pdf)).
- NurtureNet estimates newborn weight, length, head and chest circumference from low-cost phone video with a wooden ruler in frame for scale; weight MAE 114.3 g after pruning to 15 MB for offline phones ([arXiv 2405.05530](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05530)).
- Known-size circular stickers on anatomical landmarks recover 3D positions from ellipses: maximum error 0.11 cm and mean 0.058 cm against a rigid infantometer board, and within about 3 percent of board measurements on real infants ([Univ. of Washington thesis, Tang](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/50892131-ff46-4c0c-870e-33d321150281/content)). A two-camera stereo system did even better, mean absolute difference 2.0 mm from a length board ([Archives of Disease in Childhood 2014](https://adc.bmj.com/content/99/7/625)).
- Adult circumferences from front/side/back phone photos (Amazon MeasureNet, n=1200): waist-to-hip ratio MAE 0.012 to 0.017, roughly half the error of users measuring themselves. Scale comes from user-reported height and weight; training uses synthetic SMPL meshes, and the paper explicitly attributes part of its real-test error to the remaining synthetic-to-real gap ([npj Digital Medicine 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10495406/)).
- The cautionary counterexample: two consumer apps (MeThreeSixty, myBVI) against tape measurements showed precision error 0.5 to 1.9 cm but waist RMSE up to ±6.5 cm and limits of agreement of ±9 to ±12.4 cm, with systematic underestimation ([Graybeal et al., British Journal of Nutrition 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/evaluation-of-automated-anthropometrics-produced-by-smartphonebased-machine-learning-a-comparison-with-traditional-anthropometric-assessments/49AFA085C23914A64D2B89CBBD8C8BD8)). Loose clothing cost another app about 2.7 points of accuracy versus fitted clothing ([SmartFit, Electronics 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/10/11/1338)).
- Best-case smartphone pipeline: a 360-degree phone video avatar scan reports technical error of measurement about 0.5 cm (0.9 percent) across common circumferences, better than stationary booth scanners, and body-fat percentage within 3.4 to 3.5 percent MAE of DXA ([Frontiers in Medicine 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2024.1485450/full); related two-photo body fat MAE 2.16 percent in [npj Digital Medicine 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-022-00628-3)).

Reading for our use case: AP chest thickness spans roughly 18 to 30 cm. Anchored single-image systems land between about 1 and 2.5 cm end-to-end error when capture is disciplined, and sub-centimeter when the reference geometry is rigid and per-point. X-ray technique charts already group patients into thickness bands a few cm wide, so 1 to 2 cm accuracy sits inside the decision tolerance.

## 3. Scale anchoring from a known reference

The trick we plan to use, recovering absolute scale from one known distance or plane, is well precedented.

- Prompt Depth Anything takes sparse metric depth (even low-resolution LiDAR samples) as a prompt and outputs metric depth at 4K resolution from the Depth Anything backbone ([project page, Dec 2024](https://promptda.github.io/), announced in the [DA-V2 README](https://github.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2#news)). Architecturally this is our idea: inject one trusted measurement, get metric output everywhere.
- Ground-plane constraints recover metric scale in driving scenes. GroCo embeds the known ground prior in self-supervised training and beats other scale-recovery techniques on KITTI while generalizing zero-shot to DDAD ([GroCo, ECCV 2024](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/11763.pdf)); PlaneDepth represents scenes as orthogonal ground and vertical planes and extracts the ground plane unsupervised at AbsRel 0.085 ([PlaneDepth, CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Wang_PlaneDepth_Self-Supervised_Depth_Estimation_via_Orthogonal_Planes_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)); GASDA used the same constraint to adapt synthetic-trained depth to real KITTI at AbsRel about 0.114, competitive with fully supervised peers of its era ([GASDA, CVPR 2019](https://github.com/sshan-zhao/gasda)). The wall bucky plays exactly the role the road plane plays there, except ours is vertical, flat, textured, and always visible behind the patient, which is geometrically kinder.
- In SLAM, a single known camera height above the floor suffices to fix absolute map scale: ground scaling alone brought monocular translation error to 3.39 percent on KITTI odometry, and combining it with object cuboids cut it to 1.78 percent ([CubeSLAM, IEEE T-RO 2019](https://shichaoy.github.io/Publications/tro_2019_object.pdf)). Object-size priors alone achieved centimeter-level absolute trajectory error on TUM indoor sequences, though the authors note object-size references are weaker than stereo baselines or RGB-D depth ([dual-quadric scale estimation, arXiv 2202.04816](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.04816)). One precisely known plane should outperform statistical object priors.
- The applied-anthropometry papers above are all demonstrations of the same principle at cm fidelity: a credit-card-sized reference card converted to pixels-per-mm ([LAI 2024](https://pediatrics.jmir.org/2024/1/e59564/)), rulers in frame ([NurtureNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05530)), known-size stickers at each landmark ([Tang thesis](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/50892131-ff46-4c0c-870e-33d321150281/content)), and user-reported height and weight as scale priors ([MeasureNet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10495406/)).
- Metric3D v2 demonstrated metrology on internet photos, but needed EXIF focal length to do it ([paper Fig. 8](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10638254)). Webcams do not give trustworthy focal lengths, so our anchor must carry the whole scale burden rather than sharing it with metadata.

Design note worth stealing from GroCo: apply the anchor a posteriori (segment the known plane, force predicted depth onto it, rescale everything else) or a priori (train the constraint in). We start a posteriori since it needs no retraining.

## 4. Sim-to-real with synthetic humans

- BEDLAM is the strongest existence proof that synthetic-only human training transfers: networks trained purely on its Unreal-rendered SMPL-X bodies with CLO3D-simulated garments reached state-of-the-art 3D pose and shape accuracy on real benchmarks (3DPW, RICH, SSP-3D, HBW), and plain HMR trained on BEDLAM matched the then-SOTA CLIFF. Fine-tuning on real data gave only small extra gains, meaning the effective domain gap was close to zero once realism was right ([BEDLAM, CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Black_BEDLAM_A_Synthetic_Dataset_of_Bodies_Exhibiting_Detailed_Lifelike_Animated_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf), [project site](https://bedlam.is.tue.mpg.de/)). It ships 32-bit depth maps and segmentation masks alongside RGB, so it doubles as a depth-model evaluation set. Caveats: ground-truth fidelity is 2.4 mm vertex error, the BMI distribution dips exactly at 25 to 30 where the population peaks, and cloth simulation failed on some high-BMI bodies. BEDLAM 2.0 adds cameras in motion ([NeurIPS 2025](https://bedlam2.is.tue.mpg.de/)).
- BlenderProc packages the render pipeline (physics, lighting, camera sampling) that makes this reproducible for other domains ([BlenderProc, ICCV 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01911)); Unity's Perception toolkit serves the same role in-engine. Neither is human-specific; pair either with SMPL-X assets.
- Quantified gap examples: MeasureNet reports lower circumference MAE on its synthetic test set than on laser-scanned real bodies, isolating the residual synthetic-to-real gap in digital anthropometry ([npj Digital Medicine 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10495406/)). GASDA quantifies the depth-task version: synthetic-to-real adaptation with geometric constraints recovered most of the performance that real supervision provides ([CVPR 2019](https://github.com/sshan-zhao/gasda)).

For us this means a BlenderProc or UE scene with SMPL-X patients standing against a modeled bucky, rendered with realistic skin, clothing, and webcam-like noise, gives a free validation harness: we can measure plane-anchored thickness error across hundreds of body shapes before touching a patient. The BEDLAM lesson is that the harness must include clothing and motion blur or it will flatter us.

## 5. Real-time deployment in the browser

- The canonical demo is Hugging Face's transformers.js WebGPU webcam depth estimator running Depth Anything V2 Small fully client-side, defaulting to 504 px inference with a 0.4 stream scale ([live Space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/Xenova/webgpu-realtime-depth-estimation)). The ONNX conversion used by web demos (`onnx-community/depth-anything-v2-small`, fp16) is about 100 MB cached, with automatic WASM fallback when WebGPU is absent ([Web AI Showcase entry](https://paulkinlan.github.io/web-ai-showcase/models/depth-anything/)).
- onnxruntime-web documents the WebGPU execution provider path, including graph capture for static shapes and IO binding to keep tensors on the GPU ([official tutorial](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/tutorials/web/ep-webgpu.html)). UniDepth V2 added ONNX support upstream, though its license blocks most commercial use ([repo](https://github.com/lpiccinelli-eth/UniDepth)).
- TypeGPU (Software Mansion) provides typed TypeScript WebGPU abstractions with example ML pipelines and published benchmarks, useful if we hand-write preprocessing or postprocessing compute passes ([GitHub](https://github.com/software-mansion/typegpu/)).
- A production writeup integrating DA-V2-Small (q4f16) into a browser video editor stresses the operational realities: model caching, session reuse across frames, temporal frame sampling, and the fact that throughput depends heavily on GPU class, browser implementation, and resolution; it declines to publish a device-independent fps figure because none honestly exists ([dev.to, Aug 2026](https://dev.to/martindelophy/building-cinematic-depth-in-the-browser-with-depth-anything-v2-small-and-webgpu-1k07)).
- What will not run live in a browser tab: Depth Pro at 1536² costs 0.3 s on a V100; Metric3D v2's own supplementary reports 5.0 to 11.6 fps on workstation GPUs with up to 15.6 GB; diffusion-based Marigold manages 0.13 fps ([Metric3D supp table](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15506v4)). Plan on ViT-S-class models at roughly 500 px for interactive rates on laptop GPUs, verify on the actual ward hardware, and consider running every nth frame with temporal interpolation between.

## Implications for our bucky-plane anchoring plan

1. The plan is sound and precedented. Known-plane scale recovery works in driving (ground planes), SLAM (camera height), and clinical anthropometry (reference cards). A flat, permanently mounted detector plane at a calibrated distance from a fixed webcam is a cleaner reference than any of those. Expect the anchor itself to contribute millimeter-level scale error; the dominant term will be the relative-depth difference between the chest surface and the plane.
2. Model choice. Start with Depth Anything V2 Small: Apache-2.0 weights, browser-proven, fast enough for continuous guidance. Keep UniDepth V2 ViT-S as the accuracy reference offline despite CC-BY-NC licensing. Skip built-in metric heads (ZoeDepth, Metric3D, Depth Pro) as primary; they buy scale we already get from the plane, at higher compute and worse licensing.
3. Calibrate the camera once, not never. Unknown webcam intrinsics degrade every candidate model; UniDepth's whole design exists because of this. A one-time checkerboard calibration of each installed webcam (or a printed marker on the bucky face) removes the biggest documented error source.
4. Error budget. End-to-end anchored thickness targets: 1 to 2 cm median is realistic given the 1.77 to 2.47 cm infant-length results with comparable optics, improved by temporal median filtering over a short clip the way LAI averaged images. Validate against calipers across the BMI range; remember Graybeal's finding that systematic bias, not random noise, breaks these systems, and SmartFit's finding that gowns cost accuracy.
5. Build the synthetic harness first. A BlenderProc-plus-SMPL-X patient-against-panel generator lets us sweep body shapes, clothing looseness, and camera poses, and tune the plane-fitting and rescaling logic with exact ground truth. BEDLAM shows the gap can be driven near zero if we invest in clothing and sensor realism, and MeasureNet shows what happens if we don't.
6. If we ever fine-tune, prompt-style supervision fits naturally: fine-tune DA-V2 with sparse plane-distance prompts exactly as Prompt Depth Anything does with LiDAR samples.
